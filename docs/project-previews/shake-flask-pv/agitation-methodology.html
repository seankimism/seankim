Shake-flask P/V scale-up: calculations and sources

Estimate mean liquid power input per working volume, then find a receiving speed that matches it. This is a comparison of an engineering criterion; it does not establish equivalent cell stress or culture performance.

Published power model

The calculator uses the Büchs correlation for conventional unbaffled flasks, reproduced in Dinter et al., section 2.3. Its measured basis spans 100–2,000 mL nominal flasks under in-phase conditions. [1] [2]

Re = n d2 / ν

Ne′ = 70 / Re + 25 / Re0.6 + 1.5 / Re0.2

P/VL = Ne′ ρ n3 d4 / VL2/3

n
Shaking frequency in s−1; rpm / 60.
d
Maximum inner flask diameter in metres; entered mm / 1,000. Do not substitute orbit or nominal capacity.
VL
Working liquid volume in m3; entered mL × 10−6.
ρ
Liquid density in kg/m3.
ν
Kinematic viscosity in m2/s; entered mm2/s × 10−6. Dynamic viscosity μ = ρν.
P/VL
Mean liquid power input per working volume, W/m3.

The source calculation establishes the target P/V. The receiving speed is solved numerically using its own diameter and fill. The display uses the nearest whole rpm only if that setting passes the same checks; otherwise it retains the exact supported speed. The two P/V values use the displayed settings.

Orbit is absent from this power equation. Orbit enters the flow checks below. Changing orbit does not produce an independent P/V correction here. Actual flask geometry and an applicable flow regime must still be confirmed.

Conservative coverage for this first calculator

The tool accepts conventional unbaffled glass Erlenmeyer flasks and a shared Newtonian liquid. Plastic and baffled designs require an applicable vessel model or measured power data.

CheckCalculator coverage
Nominal capacity100–2,000 mL at both sites
Working fill4–20% of nominal capacity at both sites
Full orbital diameter25 or 50 mm at both sites
Speed80–380 rpm for the source and receiving match, also within receiving hardware limits
FlowPassing geometry, Fr > 0.4 and Ph ≥ 1.26

The speed, fill and orbit restrictions are conservative calculator policy drawn from the reported experimental campaign of Büchs, Lotter and Milbradt. They do not imply that every combination or commercial flask inside these bounds was validated. [3]

The defaults, ρ = 1,000 kg/m3 and ν = 1 mm2/s, represent a water-like Newtonian illustration. Enter properties at the culture temperature. The reported viscosity ranges differ by flask size: the 2001 campaign includes 1–200 mPa·s for small unbaffled 100–1,000 mL flasks, and 1 mPa·s for its larger 2–5 L group. A positive viscosity input and passing phase check do not establish coverage for every liquid/size combination. [3]

Flow checks before showing a recommendation

The phase criterion applies when orbital Froude number Fr > 0.4. Below that value, this criterion cannot classify the motion; the tool withholds a P/V recommendation. Low Fr does not prove out-of-phase flow. [4]

Fr = (2πn)2 (d0/2) / g = orbital RCF

d0 is full orbit diameter in metres; g = 9.80665 m/s2. The phase calculation uses the following relations. [2]

x = (4/π)(VL1/3/d)2, requiring 0 < x < 1

Refilm = Re (π/2) [1 − √(1 − x)]2

Ph = (d0/d) [1 + 3 log10(Refilm)]

The tool conservatively requires Ph ≥ 1.26 at source and receiving conditions. The transition is gradual; passing this screen does not validate a vessel or remove uncertainty near the transition. [2]

The 125 mL plastic reference needs separate evidence

Your earlier reference is an unbaffled plastic flask with 30 mL working volume, 120 rpm and 25 mm orbit. Its fill fraction is 24% and its orbital RCF is 0.2013. This calculator therefore withholds a P/V recommendation for it: the construction is not implemented, the fill exceeds the chosen coverage and the phase criterion is inapplicable at that Fr.

This does not mean the culture setting is unsuitable. A published 125 mL polycarbonate CFD study uses a different fill and orbit, so its result does not establish P/V for this reference. [5] Obtain the flask make/model and geometry, fluid properties, and vessel-specific measured power or a validated model. A desired 30 W/m3 remains a target, not a measurement of source power.
