Matching orbital RCF between incubators

The calculator finds the receiving-site speed that gives the same nominal orbital relative centrifugal force (RCF) as the source shaker.

Required inputs: source speed, each shaker's full orbital diameter, and the receiving equipment's speed limits. Flask construction, nominal volume, and working fill are optional records; they do not change the RCF calculation.

Orbit and speed determine nominal RCF

The orbit is the full diameter of the circular path followed by the shaker platform, not the flask diameter. [2] Its radius is half that diameter. TPP uses this radius and speed squared to calculate orbital RCF and solve for a matching speed on another orbit. [1]

RCF = (2πn / 60)2 × (D / 2000) / 9.80665

RCF
Orbital acceleration divided by standard gravity; expressed as ×g.
n
Shaking speed in rpm.
D
Full orbital diameter in mm. D / 2000 converts it to radius in metres.
9.80665
Standard gravitational acceleration in m/s2, used by this calculator.

TPP writes the same relationship with the rounded coefficient 1.118 × 10−5, radius in cm, and speed in rpm. [1] The calculator evaluates the equation above directly.

Solving for receiving speed

Setting the source and receiving RCF equal cancels the shared constants:

nreceiving = nsource × √(Dsource / Dreceiving)

  1. Enter positive source speed and full orbital diameters for both shakers.
  2. Enter the receiving equipment's minimum and maximum rpm. The minimum may be zero for a stopped setting; the maximum must be positive and at least the minimum.
  3. Calculate the exact receiving speed and check it against those equipment limits.
  4. Round to the nearest positive whole rpm only when that setting also falls within the limits. Otherwise retain the exact speed if the equipment supports it.

The calculator reports source RCF and receiving RCF at the displayed speed, plus the exact matching speed and the effect of rounding. At a whole-rpm receiving setting, the relative RCF is:

RCF ratio = (nreceiving,rounded / nsource)2 × (Dreceiving / Dsource)

Multiply this ratio by 100 for a percentage. The limits are the receiving equipment's limits; there is no flask-correlation operating envelope in this calculation.

Construction, nominal flask volume, and working fill describe the transfer conditions. They do not enter the orbital acceleration equation.

Unbaffled glass

Use the source and receiving orbit diameters and speed limits in the shared RCF equation. Flask size and fill can be recorded alongside the result.

Baffled glass

The same orbital RCF equation applies. Selecting a baffled flask adds no correction or calibration requirement to this acceleration calculation.

Unbaffled disposable / plastic

The same orbital RCF equation applies. Material and nominal volume are optional records, rather than mathematical inputs.

Baffled disposable / plastic

The same orbital RCF equation applies. Baffles and working fill do not alter the calculated acceleration of the shaker platform.

Worked example: 25 mm to 50 mm orbit

Input or resultSourceReceiving

120 × √(25 / 50) gives 84.852814 rpm. If the receiving equipment permits 85 rpm, rounding gives 0.201983 ×g, or 100.347% of the source RCF.

The exact speeds match RCF. The percentage shows the numerical effect of whole-rpm rounding.

Wave / rocking bags

Rocking motion does not follow a shaker's circular orbit. Wave bags are outside this RCF calculator and need a separate method based on their rocking motion and geometry.

What the match means

Equal orbital RCF matches nominal shaker acceleration. It does not establish equal liquid hydrodynamics, oxygen transfer, or cell performance. TPP recommends testing the effect of speed and fill for the culture. [1]
