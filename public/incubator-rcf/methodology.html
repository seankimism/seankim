Matching orbital RCF between incubators

The calculator finds the receiving-site speed that gives the same nominal orbital relative centrifugal force (RCF) as the source shaker.

Required inputs: source speed, each shaker's full orbital diameter, and the receiving equipment's speed limits.

RCF equation Worked example Limitations Sources

Orbit and speed determine nominal RCF

The orbit is the full diameter of the circular path followed by the shaker platform, not the flask diameter. [2] Its radius is half that diameter. TPP uses this radius and speed squared to calculate orbital RCF and solve for a matching speed on another orbit. [1]

RCF = (2πn / 60)2 × (D / 2000) / 9.80665

RCF
Orbital acceleration divided by standard gravity; expressed as ×g.
n
Shaking speed in rpm.
D
Full orbital diameter in mm. D / 2000 converts it to radius in metres.
9.80665
Standard gravitational acceleration in m/s2, used by this calculator.

TPP writes the same relationship with the rounded coefficient 1.118 × 10−5, radius in cm, and speed in rpm. [1] The calculator evaluates the equation above directly.

Solving for receiving speed

Setting the source and receiving RCF equal cancels the shared constants:

nreceiving = nsource × √(Dsource / Dreceiving)

  1. Enter positive source speed and full orbital diameters for both shakers.
  2. Enter the receiving equipment's minimum and maximum rpm. The minimum may be zero for a stopped setting; the maximum must be positive and at least the minimum.
  3. Calculate the exact receiving speed and check it against those equipment limits.
  4. Round to the nearest positive whole rpm only when that setting also falls within the limits. Otherwise retain the exact speed if the equipment supports it.

The calculator displays the receiving rpm, source RCF and receiving RCF at the displayed speed. Whole-rpm rounding can create a small difference between the two RCF values. The entered equipment limits determine whether a matching speed is available.

The exact speeds match RCF. The displayed RCF values reflect the small difference introduced by whole-rpm rounding.

Limitations for tech transfer

Equal orbital RCF matches nominal shaker acceleration. It does not account for flask size, geometry, working volume or liquid properties. Changing flask scale can change the liquid environment, so equal RCF alone is not sufficient to transfer a process between different flask scales. [3]

For a scale change, assess a process-relevant criterion such as power input per liquid volume (P/V, W/m3), measured mixing time or oxygen-transfer capacity where relevant. Use measurements or correlations supported for the actual vessels and operating conditions; P/V relationships have limits on the flask sizes and flow regimes studied. [4] Confirm culture performance at the receiving site.
