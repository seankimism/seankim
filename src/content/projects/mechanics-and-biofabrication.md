## Doctoral research

My dissertation, *Mechanically Informed Design Controls for Tissue Engineered Cartilage Biofabrication*, investigated the relationship between mechanical stimuli, scaffold architecture, and the behavior of cells in engineered cartilage. I worked in [Lawrence Bonassar's research group](https://bonassar.research.engineering.cornell.edu/) at Cornell University.

## Cartilage and scaffold mechanics

I investigated how local physical stimuli and mechanical instabilities influence chondrocyte behavior. This work connected the global mechanical response of porous collagen scaffolds to the local environment experienced by cells.

## Intervertebral disc supports

In related research, I designed a bioabsorbable support for tissue-engineered intervertebral discs and developed finite element models to simulate and optimize its behavior in vivo.

## Meniscus bioreactor development

I contributed to the development of an automated, closed and contained bioreactor for tissue-engineered meniscus constructs, using an electromechanical system to apply mechanical stimuli.

Related patent application: [Modular and autonomous bioreactor for tissue-engineered meniscus constructs — US 2024/0124815 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240124815A1/en), published April 18, 2024, with Charlotte Lee and Lawrence Bonassar.

These were related research efforts in cartilage, intervertebral disc supports, and meniscus bioreactors, with distinct experimental systems and design objectives.
